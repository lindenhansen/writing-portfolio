I used to carve tracks in the snow down a slope while harsh electronic beats crowded my periphery, blocking out any reminder of imbalance. Now I crave the slicing sound of metal edge meets ice and gush of wind and shouts of fellow mountain-goers.

I used to throw ranch rodeos. Now I sit by a river and pray the fish sees my white-capped ant and eats it. I wish to let it go back to the water among the pebbles and rocks, to swim away and maybe be caught by me again tomorrow.

I used to scare my sisters every time I shut the door for the night, like what hedonistic horror awaits us in the morning? Now I pick them up from the playground late at night after the stars have exposed their incandescent bodies to the stony cracks of the world.

I used to curse the thought of many miles, the huff and puff of every step. Now I sigh at the slight rise of crags—ravines light a smile in my eyes. And I can't wait until the next time I see the valley from a high, chest puffed above all the roads of my youth.

I used to peel the whites from the yellows in each hard boiled egg, hollowing the thing before I popped it into my mouth. Now I swallow it whole, curse the chalked texture, and celebrate the glossy cream.

I used to hold the heart of every tall quiet ghost, plugging along the murky bottom in their deep oceanic eyes. Now I skip past all of that.
